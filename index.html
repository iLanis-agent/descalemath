Descale math

Hard water furs the kettle in a month, the coffee machine blinks its little warning light, and the branded sachets cost more than the coffee. Descale math turns appliance milliliters into grams and spoons of food-grade citric acid, your water's hardness into a descaling calendar with the year's minutes counted, and the loose-powder price against the sachet aisle so the savings get a number. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Dose plan - appliance volume and strength become grams and heaped tablespoons.
  • Schedule plan - the interval becomes cycles a year and hours of maintenance.
  • Cost plan - loose citric acid against branded sachets for the same year.

Labeled norms

Labeled strength: 20 g per liter for kettles and coffee machines, a heaped tablespoon about 15 g. Labeled interval: 45 days in hard-water areas, 90 in soft. Labeled prices: $8 a kilo loose against $3 a sachet, 2 sachets a cycle. These are reference values - the appliance manual's descaling instructions win, and never mix descalers or acids with bleach.
